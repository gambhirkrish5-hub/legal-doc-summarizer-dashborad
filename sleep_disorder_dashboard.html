Conclusions & recommendations

  • Blood pressure and BMI are the strongest signals — both in feature importance and in standalone statistical tests (Kruskal–Wallis H ≈ 205–212, far above any other variable).
  • Age and occupation add meaningful signal, though occupation is best read as a proxy for lifestyle and shift patterns rather than a causal driver.
  • Sleep duration and quality are most useful for identifying insomnia specifically, but don't reliably separate sleep apnea from healthy sleepers.
  • Physical activity and stress are statistically significant but the weakest signals — physical activity's link to sleep apnea is confounded by occupation (e.g. nurses).
Sample size is small (374 patients) — validate on a larger external cohort before clinical use. This model should screen and triage, not diagnose; flagged patients should be referred for a formal sleep study (polysomnography). Retrain periodically and monitor for demographic drift given the current occupation skew (nurse, doctor, engineer over-represented).
